# ADR 0070: Zoom to 100 %

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-08

## Context

The design's photo toolbar starts with a zoom button reading "Fit" or "100%". Until
now the viewer only showed the whole photo, rendered from a preview level of about the
screen's size. The photo is opened from a half-size decode (`preview_source_min_edge`
1,600 px gives LibRaw's half size), so there were no full-resolution pixels to show,
and rendering a whole 24 MP photo for every edit would block interaction (CLAUDE.md §5).

## Decision

1. **Render only the visible window** (`CpuRenderer::render_window`):
   - the window's rows are rendered at the output's full width, and its columns are
     cropped;
   - stages that look around a pixel (clarity, texture, noise reduction, sharpening,
     dehaze, local masks) read the whole framed source at absolute rows, so each
     pixel is what a whole render gives there. A window is not rendered as a smaller
     photo, which would change vignetting, clarity's radius and the like;
   - **one exception:** running-sum blurs (clarity) start their sums at each chunk of
     rows, so a value may round one level (of 255) apart. Whole renders split into
     different chunks already differ the same way.
2. **Full resolution on demand** (`Engine::prepare_full`, IPC `prepare_full`):
   - the first zoom of a photo decodes it at full size on the background lane;
   - the result is kept with the open photo and counted in the open-images memory
     budget (146 MB for a 24 MP photo);
   - **until it's ready,** and while dragging (interactive quality), windows render
     from the largest preview level and are scaled up. Detail renders use the full
     resolution once it's there.
3. **IPC:**
   - `PreviewRequestDto.window` (x, y, width, height in full-resolution output pixels)
     asks for a window;
   - the frame then carries `FRAME_FLAG_WINDOW` and the part it covers (four `f32`s
     after the header; fractional when it came from a smaller level);
   - windows carry no histogram. The histogram stays that of the last whole frame.
   - Windows are cached by recipe, window and source size, and they supersede the
     viewer's other renders like any viewer frame.
4. **In the viewer:**
   - **Fit:** a click zooms to 100 % on the spot clicked.
   - **At 100 %:** one full-resolution pixel is drawn per device pixel. The photo's
     box is panned by dragging or scrolling and never uncovers an edge (it's centred
     when smaller than the viewer). The last whole frame is stretched under the
     window's render, so newly uncovered areas show at once and sharpen when their
     window arrives. A click without a drag returns to Fit.
   - **The toolbar button** ("Fit" / "100%", as designed) and **Z** toggle between
     the two.
   - **Other tools:** crop and before/after return to Fit. Retouch and masks zoom
     too, their marks drawn on the zoomed photo (see "Masks at 100 %" below). There,
     clicks and drags are the tool's: the photo pans by scrolling, or by dragging with
     Space held as in other editors, and only Z, the button or a pinch change the
     zoom. The mask toolbar, which takes the photo toolbar's place, starts with the
     same zoom button. Zoom stays at 100 % from photo to photo.
   - **Zoom levels** (added on 2026-10-08, after "it only goes from Fit to 100 %"):
     - **The range:** zoom is any scale from just above Fit to 800 %. A zoom is a
       centre and a scale, in device pixels per full-resolution pixel.
     - **Pinching** follows the fingers smoothly, keeping the point under them where
       it is. Below Fit, it fits. It's read from WebKit's gesture events, or from a
       wheel with Ctrl in other engines, and the page itself never zooms.
     - **Z, the button and a click** animate (220 ms, easing out) between Fit and
       100 %, the clicked point staying under the pointer. When 100 % is hardly
       larger than Fit, they go to twice Fit instead.
     - **⌘+ and ⌘−** animate to the next level: ⅛, ¼, ⅓, ½, ⅔, 100 %, 200 %, 400 %
       or 800 %, above Fit. ⌘0 fits.
     - **The button** shows the level ("Fit", "67%", "100%").
     - **The state** lives in `ZoomModel`, which is given its clock and frames so
       it can be tested; `useZoom` holds it for React.
   - **Windows at any zoom:** a request's `target_long_edge` is the long edge the
     whole output has at the zoom. The engine renders the window from the smallest
     source at least that sharp:
     - a preview level at or below the largest one's scale (50 % on a half-size
       preview);
     - the full resolution above it, for detail, once decoded.
     - The editor asks for the full resolution only when a zoom needs it.
5. **Removals at every size:** fills made at different sizes differ, in structure
   too (each size finds its own patches). Zooming flipped between them: the Fit view's
   level, the half-size source while dragging, and the full resolution after. So an
   open photo's removals are filled once at full resolution and shown, scaled, at
   every size.
   - **`renderer::remove::Fill`:** what the fill changed over the area it touched,
     in linear light. `apply` adds it to the same photo at any size, each pixel
     taking the average change over the full-resolution pixels it covers. At full
     size it gives exactly the filled photo; a halved photo gets the halved fill
     (within rounding).
   - **`Engine::prepare_fill`** (IPC `prepare_fill`): decodes the full resolution if
     need be, makes the fill on the background lane, and keeps it with the open photo
     for the removals it was made for. A newer call replaces one still running.
   - **Until it's ready,** renders fill at their own size as before.
     `PreviewFrame::fill_pending` (`FRAME_FLAG_FILL_PENDING`) says so, and the editor
     asks for the full-resolution fill (once per photo and set of removals), then
     renders again. The two kinds of render are cached apart.
   - **Exports of the open photo** use the fill once it's made. A full-size export
     fills at full resolution anyway, so it matches. A sized export now shows the
     removals as the viewer does, rather than filling again at its own size.
   - **The cost:** one full-size decode and fill per change to the removals, in the
     background. That's 1.4 s for a 2 % stroke on the Nikon Z 6, with the full
     resolution already decoded. Applying the fill to a preview level takes 1–3 ms.
6. **Masks at 100 %:**
   - **The tint:** at Fit it's a canvas the size of the photo on screen. At 100 %
     that would be the photo's full resolution: three or more 24 MP canvases,
     composited in the webview. So only the part in view is drawn, as a narrower crop
     of the same view, with a margin of an eighth of the view each side. The part
     snaps to a power-of-two grid, so a pan within the margin draws nothing.
   - **The brush keeps its size on screen,** as in other editors and the retouch
     brush, so zooming in paints finer strokes. A stroke's size is the brush's
     divided by the magnification.
   - **Mask resolution:** brush coverage was always rasterised at 2048 px on the
     long side, about one mask pixel to three photo pixels at 100 % on a 24 MP photo,
     too soft for the fine strokes zooming invites. Maps now follow the render's size,
     between 2048 and 8192 px. Preview levels up to 2048 px still share one map; 100 %
     windows and full-size exports get their own. The map cache is bounded by bytes
     (192 MB) as well as count.
   - **RENDERER_VERSION 5:** full-size renders and exports of brush masks change, with
     sharper edges. Recipes are unchanged.
   - **Cost:** a 2000×1200 window with a brush mask renders in 43 ms on the Nikon Z 6,
     including rasterising the mask at full size, and in 39 ms panned.
7. **Tests:**
   - **Renderer:** windows equal that part of the whole render, with every stage
     except clarity's blur, a straightened crop and a spot, in both pixel formats,
     including edges and a clamped window.
   - **Engine:** a window equals the whole full-resolution render there (within one
     level with clarity). It comes from the preview source before `prepare_full` and
     while dragging, and repeated windows are cache hits.
   - **Frontend:** zoom geometry, which covers centring, edges, whole device pixels,
     panning, zooming around a point at any scale, Fit's scale, the ⌘+ / ⌘− levels,
     and when the full resolution is needed. Frame decoding of the window.
   - **`ZoomModel`, on a hand-driven clock:** Z animates through the levels in
     between to exactly 100 % and back, and fitting at Fit does nothing. A click keeps
     its point under the pointer. A pinch is smooth, down to Fit and up to 800 %.
     ⌘+ / ⌘− step through the levels, and a pan stops an animation.
   - **Engine, sources by zoom:** 100 % and beyond use the full resolution; 50 %
     uses the half-size level, and just above 50 % the full resolution again; 25 %
     uses the next level, with the window it covers unchanged.
   - **Masks:** the visible part of the box, and a drawn part a little larger that
     stays the same for small pans (`zoom.test.ts`); maps follow the frame between
     their limits, and preview levels share one while full resolution has its own
     (`brush.rs`). In the dev mock: the tint canvas covers the view and a margin; a
     stroke painted at 100 % is the brush's size over the magnification; a pinch (as
     Ctrl-wheel) fits and zooms in, never zooming the page.
   - **Removals:** applying a fill at full size gives exactly the filled photo, and
     at half size the filled photo halved. In the engine, a render before
     `prepare_fill` is marked as a stand-in and one after isn't (never answered from
     the stand-in's cache). Over the removal, the Fit view matches the 100 % view
     halved about as closely as elsewhere, and far more closely than the stand-in
     does.
   - **Release self-test** (`removalFill`): the stand-in comes first, then whole and
     100 % frames use the full-resolution fill.
   - **Release self-test** (`zoom`): the window's source and size before and after
     the full decode, the interactive window, and Z in the editor showing a
     full-resolution window and going back to Fit.

## Consequences

- Inspecting focus and noise is possible, which the design's 100 % asks for.
- **Memory:** the first zoom on a photo costs one full-size decode (0.9 s for the
  Nikon Z 6) and its memory while the photo is open.
- **Rendering cost:** a framed edit (crop or straighten) frames the whole
  full-resolution source once per framing, about 0.1 s on the Z 6. That framing is
  cached.
- **Follow-ups:**
  - zoom levels other than 100 %;
  - a histogram that follows edits made while zoomed.
